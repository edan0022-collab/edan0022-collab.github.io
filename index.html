Archizoom’s No-Stop City (1969) imagined an endless urban landscape with no centre, boundaries or traditional hierarchy. Designed as a continuous grid, it removed traditional landmarks and created a system where every space held the same value. Today, this structure reappears digitally through endless feeds and algorithms, where freedom of movement becomes a system of constant consumption and control.

The scroll replaces the street. The feed replaces the city. Navigation once relied on choosing paths, directions and destinations. Within the digital feed, those pathways are increasingly shaped for us - guided by algorithms that decide what appears next. Are we still navigating, or are we being navigated?

REPETITION AS ARCHITECTURE.

The post is the unit. Every post occupies an equivalent cell. Same rectangle, same engagement counter.

War and shopping occupy the same visual container. Both become scrollable. The feed removes the boundaries between information, entertainment and consumption. When every moment appears in the same format, do we still recognise their differences - or do they become just another piece of content to pass by?

ABSENCE OF EXIT · CONTINUATION · NON ARRIVAL

Archizoom’s No-Stop City imagined an environment without a final destination — a continuous grid where expansion replaces arrival. In the digital state, the endless feed becomes this new landscape, where there is always another post, another update, another reason to remain.

Your gaze is an economic act.

Every second your eye rests on a pixel is logged, priced and traded. In the No-Stop State, attention becomes the resource that keeps the system expanding. Every pause, click and interaction creates value, raising the question - are we freely consuming content, or has our consumption become the product?
